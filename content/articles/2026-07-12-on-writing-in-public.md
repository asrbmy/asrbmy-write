For a long time I kept my notes private because they were not good enough. Then I noticed that nothing I was saving was getting better either.

## What changed

Publishing sets a deadline and an audience, even a tiny one. Knowing someone might read a paragraph makes me check my facts, cut the waffle and finish the thought. That is most of the benefit, and it does not require many readers.

## The things I was afraid of

I worried about being wrong in public. It has happened, and it was fine. A correction, clearly marked, is more useful to readers than silence, and it is how I learned the most.

I worried nobody would care. Mostly nobody does, at first, and that is freeing. You get time to find your voice before anyone expects one.

## Small rules I follow

- Say what I know and how I know it. Label guesses as guesses.
- Add an "Updated" date when I change something important.
- Write for one specific person, usually past me.
- Link to the sources I used, so readers can check.
- Publish before it feels ready. A finished imperfect post beats a perfect draft.

## Writing as thinking

The surprise has been that writing is not only a way to share what I think. It is how I find out what I think. A fuzzy idea becomes clear the moment I try to put it in a sentence, and many ideas turn out not to survive that test.

## If you are hesitating

Start with something small: one useful thing you learned this week. Put a date on it, publish it, and write the next one. The habit matters more than any single post.

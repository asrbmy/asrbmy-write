I have tried many note-taking apps. What lasted was not an app at all; it was a few habits and a folder of text files.

## Why plain text

Markdown files open anywhere, search instantly, and survive any software company going away. They also work nicely with Git, which gives me history for free.

## Two kinds of notes

I separate what a source says from what I think about it.

- **Source notes** summarise one paper, article or talk in my own words, with the reference at the top.
- **Idea notes** hold a single claim or question, written so I can understand it later without the source open. They link to the source notes that support them.

## A template for source notes

```markdown
# Title of the source

- Author / year:
- Link:
- Why I read it:

## Main claim
One or two sentences.

## Evidence
What they showed, and how strong it is.

## My questions
What is missing or unclear?
```

The "Why I read it" line matters most. Without it, notes turn into summaries nobody needs.

## Write in your own words

Copying a quote feels productive but teaches nothing. Restating a paragraph in a sentence forces you to find out whether you understood it.

## The weekly review

Once a week I spend twenty minutes reading the notes I made, linking related ideas and deleting anything I would not miss. This is where the connections appear, and it keeps the folder from becoming a pile.

## Keep it light

If a system needs more effort than the thinking it supports, simplify it. A rough note you actually wrote beats a perfect one you postponed.

You do not need to become a security expert to browse more privately. A handful of habits, applied consistently, do most of the work.

## 1. Keep your browser updated

Updates fix security holes that are actively exploited. Turn on automatic updates and restart the browser when it asks.

## 2. Use fewer extensions

Every extension can read the pages you visit. Keep only the ones you truly use, install them from the browser's official store, and remove anything you no longer remember adding.

## 3. Turn on HTTPS-only mode

Most modern browsers can refuse to load pages over plain HTTP, or at least warn you first. This stops anyone on the same network from reading or altering what you send.

## 4. Separate your activities with profiles

Use one browser profile for banking and important accounts, and another for casual browsing. Cookies and logins stay separate, so a tracker on a recipe site cannot see your other life.

## 5. Control cookies and trackers

Block third-party cookies, and consider clearing site data when you close the browser. Expect to sign in more often; that is the trade-off.

## 6. Use a password manager

Unique passwords for every site limit the damage when one service is breached. A manager also refuses to autofill on look-alike domains, which helps against phishing.

## 7. Think before you click "Sign in with…"

Social sign-in is convenient, but it ties your accounts together. For services you do not fully trust, create a separate account with its own email address.

## What these tips do not do

None of this makes you anonymous. Your internet provider, the sites you log in to, and your own habits all still reveal things. The aim is to reduce needless tracking, which is a reasonable goal even if perfect privacy is not.
